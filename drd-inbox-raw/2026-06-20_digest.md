#### Core Development & Rationale

New York State's All-Electric Buildings Act (AEBA) took effect January 1, 2026, making New York the first state in the nation to legally prohibit fossil fuel mechanical equipment in most new building construction. The law covers all new construction up to seven stories immediately, with full expansion to every new building in 2029. A federal court ruling cleared the final legal challenge to the law, and grandfathering is narrowly limited to building permit applications deemed 'substantially complete' before January 1, 2026 — meaning the vast majority of new projects must now be designed and specified under the all-electric standard. #### Key Industry Impacts

* **Real Estate**: Developers of new residential buildings up to seven stories must fully budget for all-electric MEP systems — heat pumps, induction cooking, electric domestic hot water — versus traditional gas infrastructure, with typical upfront cost premiums of 2–8% depending on building type and climate zone; however, IRA Section 48D clean energy investment tax credits and NY Green Bank financing mechanisms can materially offset this premium for projects meeting income-restriction or sustainability thresholds, making all-electric pro formas increasingly competitive at scale; the specific exemptions for manufacturing, hospitals, and commercial food establishments require careful MEP scope definition on mixed-use projects to avoid compliance ambiguity at permit submission.
* **Engineering & Construction**: MEP engineering teams must now design NYC and New York State new-build HVAC and plumbing entirely without gas connections: air-source or ground-source heat pumps replace gas boilers for space heating, VRF systems or chilled-water air handlers replace gas-fired rooftop units, and heat-pump water heaters or electric resistance systems replace gas-fired DHW equipment; grid capacity relief (the 18-month rule) is available only in genuinely underserved utility service areas, and engineering teams should not assume this exemption will be available in most NYC metro locations without explicit utility confirmation.
