#### Core Development & Rationale
Construction is underway on The Willow, a six-story, 36-unit 100% affordable senior housing development at 1033 Willow Avenue in Hoboken, New Jersey — the first fully affordable housing building to be constructed in the city in over 30 years. Developed by Community Investment Strategies through a multi-year partnership with the City of Hoboken's Department of Community Development, the project transforms a formerly city-owned surface parking lot that sat idle after the devastating 1973 Willow Avenue fire, which claimed the lives of 11 residents of a tenement that once occupied the site. The development will deliver 36 deed-restricted affordable apartments for residents aged 55 and older (3 studios, 28 one-bedrooms, 5 two-bedrooms), alongside a ground-floor community activity room and a fifth-floor library, with completion expected Fall 2027. The groundbreaking drew Mayor Emily Jabbour, Senator Raj Mukherji, and Congressman Rob Menendez — reflecting both the political significance of the milestone and the difficulty of delivering affordable senior housing in one of New Jersey's most supply-constrained and expensive waterfront cities. The project is selected today because it demonstrates a replicable public-private model for unlocking deeply affordable units in land-scarce, high-cost municipalities through long-term city-owned site conversion.

#### Key Industry Impacts
* **Real Estate**: The Willow demonstrates that municipalities in supply-constrained Hudson County can convert dormant city-owned parcels into deed-restricted affordable senior housing through developer partnerships, establishing a template that Hoboken, Jersey City, and other high-cost NJ waterfront cities can replicate — particularly as the state and federal affordable housing pipeline faces funding uncertainty and local land is nearly exhausted for traditional site acquisition.
* **Engineering & Construction**: The project's commitment to Zero Energy Ready Home design standards and ENERGY STAR certification on a 6-story elevator building sets a high-performance benchmark for affordable multifamily construction in New Jersey, requiring careful mechanical system selection, building envelope detailing, and commissioning that will carry cost and schedule implications for future affordable senior housing projects in the region.
